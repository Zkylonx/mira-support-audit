The audit covered Adele, Coco D and Symmetry from 23:53 on 25 November to 00:53 on 26 November 2026, Istanbul time. The owner requested a review of the match between guest guides, picture workbooks and Mira’s WhatsApp support in English, Turkish, German, French and Russian.

Which changes deserve attention first

In simulated compound emergencies, Mira sometimes offered ordinary equipment troubleshooting. A correction could still return a picture of the wrong equipment. A change of boat could leave the previous yacht selected. Each issue can produce a plausible answer that directs a guest to the wrong action or boat.

All 1,097 existing release tests passed. The new question set exercised situations outside that suite. The findings describe what the released application did under the stated test conditions. Ordinary live answer quality and the actual installations aboard the yachts still require separate verification.

This is a public review copy of the owner audit. It includes fleet comparisons, documented discrepancies, language results and aggregated evidence. Guest transcripts, private source links, exact safety-control access details and step-by-step failure reproductions are held in the owner report.

Questions to resolve against vessel documents

Symmetry’s guest guide lists 250 L of fuel, consistent with the operator-confirmed figure in Mira. A separate boat sheet, picture workbook and public listing give 350 L. The guide gives 15.29 m overall, the public listing gives 14.30 m and the separate boat sheet gives 12.90 m. Dufour’s brochure lists 15.29 m overall and 14.32 m hull length.

Symmetry is marketed with eight berths. The workbook contains a six-person licence entry and describes three doubles and one single, or seven sleeping positions. Reservations needs the current vessel documents and an as-fitted berth plan. Adele’s records also disagree about additional crew berths.

Coco D’s guide prints 3 × 300 A for its lithium house bank. Three 300 Ah batteries yield 900 Ah of nominal capacity. A is current and Ah is capacity. The source records also disagree about inverter power and the identification of adjacent electrical controls. A charger nameplate alone cannot predict charging time.

The guests’ actual questions show where equipment descriptions need more depth. A switch location does not resolve a recurring fault. A fuel-gauge answer does not explain an engine that will not start. Knowing which tap draws tank water does not establish whether the water is suitable to drink.

Test conditions and recommended checks

The production simulation endpoint was disabled. The audit ran code matching deployed v221 with in-memory WhatsApp delivery and a snapshot of published equipment records. Production AI/access values were withheld. The primary test assumed guest access and guest AI; two additional profiles examined alternative settings. No guest or staff messages were sent.

Four compound danger scenarios missed the emergency flow in 60/60 trials when the external classifier was unavailable. Supplying an affirmative emergency flag selected the localized emergency flow in 60/60. These controlled results show a dependency on recognition of danger. They do not give a production incident rate.

Recommended verification order

First verify urgent-message recognition, topic corrections and the selected boat before technical advice. Next reconcile disputed source values with the vessel papers and the installed equipment. Have a crew member sign off each safety-critical record and its boat-specific cartoon. Finally verify the guest journey and rerun provider-enabled trials with reviewers in each language.

Eight repeated test waves produced the same reply signature. The selected boat persisted at one minute and at 23 h 59 min of simulated inactivity and cleared at 24 h 01 min. Session ages were injected test values.
